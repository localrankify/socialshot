How to clip on Twitch, and turn your clips into Shorts

The Alt+X shortcut, clipping from a VOD, how long a Twitch clip can be, and the step most people skip: making the clip work on Shorts, Reels and TikTok.

SocialShot team17 Nov 20263 min read

Twitch clips are the fastest way to save a great moment from a stream. They are also where most people stop, with a horizontal 30-second clip that never leaves Twitch. This guide covers both halves: how to clip on Twitch, and how to turn those clips into Shorts, Reels and TikToks that people outside Twitch will see.

How to clip on Twitch on desktop

  1. Open a live stream in your browser.
  2. Press Alt+X (Windows) or Option+X (Mac), or click the clip icon in the player controls.
  3. A clip editor opens in a new tab with the last part of the stream.
  4. Drag the handles to trim the clip. Twitch clips can be between 5 and 60 seconds.
  5. Add a title and press Publish. You get a link to share.

Because Twitch keeps a short buffer of the stream, you can press the shortcut just after the moment happens. You do not have to predict it.

How to clip on Twitch on mobile

In the Twitch app, tap the stream to show the controls, then tap the clip icon. Trim the clip, give it a title and share it. The editing options are simpler than on desktop.

How to clip a Twitch VOD

If the streamer saves past broadcasts, open the VOD, move to the moment and use the clip button on the player. This is how most editors and clippers work: they go through the VOD after the stream and pull out the best parts. It is reliable, but it means watching hours of video back.

Streamers can limit who is allowed to clip their channel, for example only followers. If the clip button is missing, the channel may have clips turned off.

The problem with Twitch clips on other platforms

A Twitch clip is horizontal, has no captions, and starts wherever the person clipping pressed the button. On YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok, that clip shows up as a small box with black bars, silent for most viewers. It rarely gets watched to the end.

To work on short-form platforms, a clip needs to be:

  • Vertical (9:16), with your face cam or the action filling the frame.
  • Captioned, because most people watch without sound at first.
  • Tight, starting near the payoff and ending on the reaction.

Tips for better Twitch clips

  • Clip the reaction, not just the play. The second after the moment, when you or chat react, is often the best part.
  • Keep it short. 20 to 45 seconds is enough for most moments.
  • Title it like a search. Say what happens and in which game, for example "GTA 6 chase ends in the river".
  • Post the same day. Moments age fast, especially in big launches like GTA 6.
