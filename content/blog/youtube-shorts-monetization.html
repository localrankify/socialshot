YouTube Shorts gets over 200 billion views a day, according to YouTube, and it is the first place many creators earn from short clips. On 10 August 2026 YouTube announced the biggest change to its Partner Program since 2018, and it lands on 1 February 2027. Here is how Shorts monetization works now, what changes, and what to do in the months before.

How Shorts money works today

Shorts are not paid like long videos, where ads run inside your own video. Shorts ads play between Shorts in the feed, so YouTube pools the money:

  1. Ad revenue from the Shorts feed goes into a Creator Pool.
  2. Part of it pays for music used in Shorts.
  3. The rest is split between monetizing creators by their share of eligible Shorts views.
  4. You keep 45% of your share.
The 45% figure is often misread. It is 45% of the part of the pool your views earned, not 45% of all ad money around your Short. That is why Shorts pay far less per view than long videos.

The thresholds today

  • Long-form route: 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months.
  • Shorts route: 1,000 subscribers and 10 million valid Shorts views in the last 90 days.

YouTube says creators already in the program are not held to the new entry bar. Reports say they need to accept the updated terms by 31 January 2027, and that a channel below 10 million qualified Shorts views in a rolling 90-day window stops getting a Creator Pool share until it climbs back above. Fan funding and shopping keep their lower thresholds. Check YouTube's Help Center article on the changes for the exact definitions, since outlets have described the terms slightly differently.

What this means for clippers and streamers

  • Apply before February if you are close. If you are near 10 million Shorts views or 4,000 watch hours, getting in under the current rules is much easier.
  • Volume alone is no longer enough. A rolling 90-day test rewards channels that post steadily, every week, not in bursts.
  • Original edits matter more. Unedited reposts are not eligible. Clip your own streams and podcasts, or content you have permission to use, and edit every post.
  • Use Shorts to grow long-form. Shorts views do not count toward watch hours, but a good Short sends viewers to your full streams and videos.

Post enough Shorts without burning out

The channels that hit Shorts thresholds post a lot, and almost all of it comes from long content they already made. A YouTube Shorts maker turns each stream or episode into several vertical clips with word-by-word captions, so one long video fills a week of Shorts.
